What We Believe

This is what we believe.

We stand on the confessions the church has held for centuries — with the gospel at the center.

The four pillars

  1. 01

    His StoryThe gospel at the center

    The great story of the Lord who saved us. Jesus Christ stands at the center of everything we do.

  2. 02

    My StoryA life that witnesses

    The story you live out in the world. We are not an audience — we are living witnesses.

  3. 03

    Deep StoryFormed by Word and prayer

    The story that grows deeper inside. Each person is formed into a faithful disciple.

  4. 04

    Our StoryA faithful community

    The story we finish together, showing the character of Christ where we actually live.

The Reformed confessions

The Belgic Confession (1561) · The Heidelberg Catechism (1563) · The Canons of Dort (1619)

Coming from a Presbyterian church? The roots are the same. Korean Presbyterians use the Westminster Confession; Reformed churches use the Heidelberg Catechism. Two confessions from the same Reformation trunk — what we believe is not different.

The Heidelberg Catechism

Written in 1563 and recited by Reformed churches ever since. A few of its questions, in brief.

Q 1

What is your only comfort in life and in death?

That I am not my own. Body and soul, in life and in death, I belong to my faithful Savior Jesus Christ.

Q 2

What must you know to live and die in that comfort?

Three things: how deep my sin runs, how I have been delivered from it, and how to live in gratitude for that deliverance.

Q 21

What is true faith?

Knowing as true what God has revealed, and at the same time trusting deeply that He has done it for me as well.

Q 32

Why are you called a Christian?

Because by faith I belong to Christ. So I carry His name and share in His work.

Q 116

Why do Christians need to pray?

Because prayer is the heart of the gratitude God asks of us, and because He gives His grace to those who ask.

These are short paraphrases, not the official text. Read the full catechism at CRCNA ↗.

This confession is also why the church is called Story. When your story stops being your own and becomes His, that is where the story begins.
